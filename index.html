01
FOUNDATION

Introduction

The CMOS inverter is one of the most fundamental building blocks in modern digital integrated circuits. It consists of two complementary MOSFETs: a PMOS transistor connected to the positive supply voltage and an NMOS transistor connected to ground. The two devices work together to produce an output that is the logical complement of the input.

Although the CMOS inverter appears simple, its electrical behaviour provides important insights into digital circuit design. Static analysis allows us to understand the relationship between the input voltage and output voltage, determine transistor operating regions, estimate noise margins, and identify the switching behaviour of the circuit.

⚡
Key Idea

A CMOS inverter converts a LOW input into a HIGH output and a HIGH input into a LOW output while ideally consuming almost no static power.

02
CIRCUIT STRUCTURE

CMOS Inverter

A CMOS inverter uses complementary transistors. The PMOS transistor forms the pull-up network while the NMOS transistor forms the pull-down network. Their gates are connected together and act as the input terminal.

CMOS INVERTER Complementary MOS Logic
↑

PMOS

Pulls the output toward VDD when the input is LOW, producing a logic HIGH.

↓

NMOS

Pulls the output toward ground when the input is HIGH, producing a logic LOW.

⇄

Complementary Action

Ideally, one transistor is OFF while the other is ON during the stable logic states.

STATIC ANALYSIS

Operating Regions

Region 1 — Input is LOW

When the input voltage is close to 0 V, the NMOS transistor is turned OFF because its gate-to-source voltage is below the threshold voltage. At the same time, the PMOS transistor is turned ON. It connects the output node to VDD, making the output voltage approximately equal to the supply voltage.

Region 2 — Transition Region

As VIN increases, the NMOS transistor begins conducting while the PMOS transistor continues to conduct. Both transistors may be ON simultaneously. The output voltage starts decreasing rapidly. This region determines the switching characteristics and is particularly important when analysing the voltage transfer characteristic.

Region 3 — Input is HIGH

When VIN approaches VDD, the NMOS transistor is strongly ON and provides a low-resistance path to ground. The PMOS transistor becomes OFF. Consequently, the output voltage approaches 0 V, representing a logic LOW.

CORE CONCEPT

Voltage Transfer Characteristic

The Voltage Transfer Characteristic, commonly called the VTC, is a graph of output voltage VOUT versus input voltage VIN. It provides a visual representation of how the CMOS inverter transforms an input signal into its complementary output.

05
DIGITAL RELIABILITY

Noise Margins

The VTC is also useful for understanding the noise tolerance of a CMOS inverter. Noise margins indicate how much unwanted voltage disturbance can be tolerated while still correctly interpreting a signal as a logic HIGH or LOW.

NMH

High Noise Margin

Represents the maximum noise voltage that can be added to a HIGH-level signal without causing an incorrect logic interpretation.

NML

Low Noise Margin

Represents the amount of noise that can be added to a LOW-level signal while maintaining reliable logic operation.

06
WHY CMOS?

Advantages of CMOS Inverter

01

Low Static Power

Ideally, there is no direct DC path from VDD to ground in the stable logic states.

02

High Noise Immunity

CMOS circuits provide good noise margins, making them reliable for digital systems.

03

High Packing Density

CMOS technology enables a huge number of transistors to be integrated on a single chip.

04

Rail-to-Rail Output

The output can approach both supply rails, giving strong logic HIGH and LOW levels.

FINAL TAKEAWAY

The inverter is simple. The analysis is powerful.

Static analysis of a CMOS inverter provides the foundation for understanding much more complex digital VLSI circuits. By studying the relationship between VIN and VOUT, we can determine transistor operating regions, switching voltage, voltage gain, and noise margins.

The Voltage Transfer Characteristic therefore acts as a powerful tool for evaluating the performance and reliability of CMOS logic. From a basic inverter to processors containing billions of transistors, the principles observed here remain fundamental to modern VLSI design.
